How the clock works

Choose the German DB face or the Swiss railway face by swiping left or right on the clock. The seconds hand completes its lap in 58 seconds for DB or 58.5 seconds for Swiss, then waits at twelve until the next minute. Your clock remembers the selected theme. The firmware gets UTC time from pool.ntp.org and applies your selected timezone.

The firmware and this installer are on GitHub. You can build them yourself, change the clock, or report a bug. The original code uses the MIT license; Railway branding and third-party components have separate terms.

Connection or installation trouble?
  • Close serial monitors and the installer dialog before opening clock setup. Only one connection can use the USB port at a time.
  • If the board is missing from the picker, try a USB data cable. To enter download mode, hold BOOT while resetting the board, then release BOOT.
  • If Wi-Fi does not connect, check the network name, password, and 2.4 GHz availability.
  • Without Wi-Fi, the clock continues locally. Its RTC keeps time across resets while it remains powered.
Hardware, accuracy & development

Verified on V2 hardware with CO5300 display and CST820-family touch. The 180 ms minute-hand advance is an adjustable approximation. Frame rate varies with hand angle; this firmware does not sustain 60 fps. Source builds use ESP-IDF v5.5.2.
